## Informal specifications: what improved and what did not

- **The main loss mechanism is identified.** B1 correctly connects `totalVaultShares()` reading the live tToken balance, donation-inflated conversions, and zero-share victim deposits. S4 requires donation-neutral pricing; S5 requires immediate deposited value within **10 USDC base units**. The substantive improvement over F1 is explicit protective specification: F1 already recognized donation harm in B-5, but its property set contained no corresponding depositor-protection obligation. See [F1 discovery review](F1.md#discovery-and-interpretation).
- **The improvement is broader protection, not merely stronger zero-share language.** Original F0 P8 and T1's non-dust donation requirement already targeted zero-share losses. S5 also rejects deposits receiving positive shares worth materially less than the assets paid. T02 supplied the same economic intent and reported a positive-share loss over epsilon, though its original 358-unit prediction and later 349-unit report remain unresolved. Compare the [T02 originals and findings](T2.md#concrete-t2-findings), rather than treating F02's zero-share PoC as the first detection of the bug.
- **Credit the supplied intent correctly.** S4/S5 were given in L2; they are successful retention of input requirements, not independent discovery by the F02 agent. F0 and T1 inferred narrower protections without those explicit instructions, while F1 omitted them. The available pilots therefore do not show monotonically improving discovery with more documentation. They support the narrower observation that explicit economic requirements improve consistent construction of relevant checks.
- **Added-property counts do not equal independent discoveries.** I1 specializes the supplied fair-deposit concern to zero shares; I8 restates the donation concern in tToken-backing units. Both are useful diagnostic refinements. I7 adds a cross-contract parity obligation and I5 asks about actual advertised capacity, but these must be evaluated separately from the supplied S4/S5 translation. The [properties](../runs/F2/PROPERTIES.md) explicitly distinguish additions before and during investigation.
- **The economic interpretation repeats the earlier error.** [B1's impact paragraph](../runs/F2/REPORT.md) concludes that offset-zero attacks are griefing because the attacker loses money in its one-victim example. That example establishes one unprofitable sequence, not a universal no-profit result. Reference calibration found profitable multi-victim sequences, including two equal victims with a larger attacker seed; F0's later human-prompted report describes **+110.049998 USDC** in a two-victim EVM execution. See [calibration](findings-log.md#calibration-reference-tests-before-any-agent-run) and the [separate F0 follow-up](F0.md#final-p8-exploit-follow-up-reported-by-petar--2026-10-27). Those earlier outcomes do not become unprompted F02 successes.

## CVL translation: matched guarantees and concrete gaps

- **S5 is a good translation of the central guarantee.** [`fairDeposit`, lines 210–219](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L210), executes `shares = deposit(...)`, then `worth = previewRedeem(..., shares)`, and asserts `to_mathint(worth) >= assets - EPSILON()`. Epsilon is in assets, valuation is immediate, and no share-price cap or backing-equals-supply assumption removes the donation-inflated state. `setupLinks()` bounds supplies/rate but retains the submitted concrete witness's range. Normal calls prune reverts, appropriate for this successful-deposit guarantee; they do not prove deposit availability. This was the initial source-level assessment; the later follow-up reports failure on a revised model, with reachability review still required.
- **S4 directly models the external action.** [`donationDoesNotMovePrice`, lines 181–190](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L181), records `sharePrice`, executes `ttoken.transfer(e, currentContract, amount)`, and asserts unchanged price. It expresses the supplied requirement without equating donated and accounted balances. The later follow-up reports this rule failing after model revisions; see its result and reachability qualifications below.
- **Some competent translations are equivalent to earlier ones.** [`depositMatchesPreview` and `redeemMatchesPreview`, lines 376–403](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L376), check actual receiver balance deltas against return values and preview lower bounds. The shared balance-delta relations already exist in F0/F1, so rewriting them is not a translation improvement. F02 retains selected receiver exclusions. [`pausedWrapReverts`/`pausedUnwrapReverts`, lines 145–164](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L145), cover basic and slippage overloads, as earlier formal stages did; this is broader overload coverage than T02's submitted basic-operation pause checks. See the [matched F0/F1 analysis](F0-F1-matched-cvl.md).
- **Authorization is a real regression against the shared informal promise.** S19 says unauthorized actors cannot perform privileged actions. [`pauseOnlyByPauseUnpause`, lines 424–430](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L424), asserts only `before != after => selector is pause or unpause`; the rewards-proxy rule likewise checks the setter's selector. Neither relates successful calls to caller permission. A permissionless `pause()` can satisfy that assertion. `canCall` is `NONDET`, and `claimRewards` is excluded. F0/F1 had explicit `successful restricted call => authorized` obligations. F02's manual inspection of `restricted` is useful but does not supply the missing CVL claim. A mutation check is proposed below, not an observed result.
- **I1's deposit domain is weakened relative to its words.** The [informal I1](../runs/F2/PROPERTIES.md) covers every nonzero deposit, but [`depositNeverMintsZero`, lines 222–229](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L222), requires `assets > EPSILON()`, omitting 1–10 base units. That may be a sensible intended dust exception; it is absent from the sentence being translated. Record the mismatch instead of silently normalizing the requirement.
- **I5 is incomplete on redemption availability.** [I5](../runs/F2/PROPERTIES.md) covers both maximums, but [`depositUpToMaxDepositSucceeds`, lines 322–332](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L322), is the only actual-operation availability rule. `redeemRespectsMaxRedeem` checks rejection *above* a limit, not success *within* it. Thus no returned rule establishes the advertised redemption-capacity claim; F1 did attempt owner redemption availability, albeit with its own narrower-domain gap.
- **S13 checks a necessary consequence, not full rounding semantics.** [`conversionRoundTripRoundsDown`, lines 369–373](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L369), asserts only `convertToAssets(convertToShares(a)) <= a`. This does not independently establish each conversion's prescribed floor relative to its reference value. Mutually consistent conversion errors can cancel. Like F1's conversion-only round trip, it also does not execute deposit/redeem transitions; compare [the different F0/F1 informal claims](F0-F1-matched-cvl.md).
- **I4 exposes an input-domain problem in the expected “holds” assessment.** [`wrapperUsdcBalanceUnchanged`, lines 460–469](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L460), excludes the wrapper as caller but not as redemption receiver. A user can intentionally redeem to the wrapper; incoming redeemed USDC then remains there after a self-transfer. That contradicts the unrestricted unchanged-balance property without establishing theft. This is a source-derived scenario, not a returned prover counterexample. It illustrates why apparently harmless self-recipient cases matter when interpreting a universal claim.

## Model fidelity and comparison with T02

- **F02 simplifies relevant behavior more than F1.** The [unchanged F1 mock](../runs/F1/F01_TTokenMock.sol) computes the exchange rate from `(cash + borrows - reserves) / supply`, models timestamp accrual, and includes selected guardian/cap gates. F02's [mock](../runs/F2/certora/harness/TTokenMock.sol#L30) stores an independent rate, makes accrual a no-op, and permits only rate increases through `accrue(newRate)`. F1 remains partial and has documented wrong error codes; nevertheless F02 removes additional behavior. A more focused model can retain the wrapper donation bug while losing other integration failures. Trusting Takara does not prove those omissions preserve every property being assessed.
- **The difficult accrual branch is bypassed by construction.** F02's `accrualBlockTimestamp()` always returns `block.timestamp`, so the wrapper's `_currentExchangeRate` early return always applies in this model. I7 is explicitly manual-only. `priceNeverDecreasesOnAccrual` tests a model whose `accrue` already requires `newRate >= exchangeRateStored`; it does not establish that real Takara always supplies such a transition. This is an environment restriction, distinct from a correctly shaped CVL inequality.
- **A concrete model exclusion explains the apparent price-monotonicity disagreement.** [T02's returned summary](../runs/T2/SUMMARY.md) reports **1,043,481 → 1,000,000** when the entire Takara market empties, with no wrapper holders affected. Its [test](../runs/T2/WrapperProperties.t.sol#L78) performs the market-draining sequence. Actual Takara source returns `initialExchangeRateMantissa` at zero supply. F02's stored-rate mock lacks that reset, and its parametric operation set is the wrapper, not all external market actions. Its expected S2 “holds” therefore cannot rebut T02's boundary result or establish the README's full reachable-sequence claim.
- **B2/B3/B4 have different evidential status from the B1 arithmetic witness.** [B2](../runs/F2/REPORT.md) identifies huge-input checked-multiplication overflow and acknowledges ERC-4626 permits overflow on unreasonable inputs; this is a discrepancy with the literal README, not automatically a practical exploit. [B3](../runs/F2/REPORT.md) discusses real policy/liquidity limits absent in part from the mock; only deposit availability is formalized. [B4](../runs/F2/REPORT.md) is explicitly a conditional, allegedly unreachable payout/return mismatch. Do not count these as four equally executed or formally confirmed vulnerabilities.
- **Toolchain and state assumptions limit comparison.** The report records CLI 8.20.3 rather than planned 8.20.2, npm dependency downloads without recorded Soldeer hash verification, and default solc EVM settings instead of the incompatible Cancun setting. It mentions `git log` despite intended source-only isolation; no isolation verdict is inferred. `setupLinks()` is not a reachability proof or full initialization/dependency invariant. Supply bounds alone do not constrain every balance mapping. The configuration also enables optimistic loops and basic rule sanity. Any future pass must retain those qualifications and be checked against actual call resolution and reached approximations.

- **Removing impossible token states is useful, but supporting assumptions need validation.** [Section 3.3](../runs/F2/FOLLOWUP_REPORT_F02.md) reports balances near `2^256` with small/zero supply causing unchecked-addition artefacts. - **The reported donation witness has a specific reachability warning.** [B1](../runs/F2/FOLLOWUP_REPORT_F02.md) describes 999,999 outstanding share units and a two-unit tToken donation moving a whole-share quote **0 → 1 USDC base unit**. Under the unchanged offset-zero mock, ordinary wrap/deposit/redeem/unwrap transitions from empty preserve `V ≥ S`, where `V` is wrapper tToken backing and `S` is share supply. Minted shares round down; exits round down in the remaining vault's favor. Consequently `floor(10^6 * (V+1)/(S+1)) ≥ 10^6`, so the stated rate floor `1e16` implies a whole-share asset quote **≥ 10,000**, not zero. This source-derived argument suggests that exact witness starts from an unreachable backing state. It is not a replay of the unavailable trace and does not invalidate the known donation bug or the earlier explicit 500-USDC sequence. A supporting inequality may retain donation states; assuming `V == S` would exclude them and repeat the earlier masking failure.

## Place in the research and focused follow-up

- **Hypothesis 1: general intent versus mechanism-specific checks.** S5 protects the depositor without naming donations; S4 and inferred I8 name the mechanism. Their coexistence makes a useful comparison with F0/T1's narrower zero-share checks. F02 supports the usefulness of supplying general intent, but its concrete witness is zero-share only. A positive-share failing counterexample would add direct evidence for S5's broader coverage.
- **Hypothesis 2: property and scope before method.** T02 runs actual Takara source with explicit donation actions; F02 writes general CVL over a simplified external model and supplies an integer witness. Both address the same loss requirement. The follow-up reports violations after model revisions, but missing raw results, different external models and no timing measurement prevent a matched comparison of detection speed or success. The empty-market reset is a concrete example of a method comparison being confounded by model scope.
- **Hypothesis 3: distinguish translation from interpretation.** Asset-denominated S5 is a good translation, while S19's permission omission and I1/I5's domain gaps show that compiling CVL can still express incomplete guarantees. Repeated griefing-only conclusions across stages challenge confidence in economic interpretation. The initial report saw no prover counterexample; the later follow-up now permits review of its reported counterexample triage, which fixes several model artefacts but still overstates authorization and griefing-only impact. The project's claim that trust moves to specifications and models has concrete support here, without establishing that AI is generally good or bad at either task.
- **Observed synthesis.** F02 identifies the main victim-loss mechanism, retains supplied economic intent and writes relevant rules. It does not independently discover those supplied requirements, establish profitable exploitation, or demonstrate globally superior CVL. Its follow-up reports formal counterexamples on a revised model, subject to the evidence and reachability limits above. Better central properties coexist with an authorization regression and a narrower external model. One unscored pilot per available condition, differing prompts/models and unconfirmed execution/isolation preclude statistical rates or a general method ranking.
- **Original evaluation plan, now followed up.** The initial recommendation was to run `totalSupplyIsSumOfBalances` (supporting invariant), `donationDoesNotMovePrice`, `fairDeposit`, and `depositMatchesPreview` as individually recorded jobs. The original expectations were pass/fail/fail/pass; those outcomes are now reported after model revisions, not independently verified here. The contrast tests whether accounting/preview consistency survives economic harm. Preserve original spec/config, job URLs, full results, sanity outcomes, runtime and counterexamples; verify the invariant rather than treating it as established merely because another rule assumes it. Triage arbitrary backing states against a reachable donation sequence.
- **Separately labeled evaluator follow-ups, still unreported.** If `fairDeposit` finds only zero shares, restrict a copied rule to positive returned shares and test whether meaningful loss remains. In a different disposable copy, remove `restricted` from `pause()` and rerun `pauseOnlyByPauseUnpause` with sanity evidence; a surviving pass would demonstrate the permission gap. Neither revision belongs to original F02 scoring. These are proposed evaluations, not authorization to alter frozen prompts or the shared template. Further overflow or simplified-accrual jobs are lower priority for this research question.
