# Experiment 01: final findings and remediation insight

> **Reviewer notes, not agent output.** Written by the orchestrator during the research, from the run artifacts in [`../runs`](../runs). Copied from the private research repository; links to private material (evaluation project, template, prover jobs, sessions) were removed and file links point to this folder. See [the index](README.md).

## Main conclusion

- **The agent could propose a relevant root-cause mitigation before understanding the most damaging exploit.** T02's initial report and F02's original report recommend internal tToken accounting while still calling the attack griefing rather than theft. This is the missing distinction in the earlier synthesis: remediation insight was sometimes better than economic interpretation.
- **That recommendation was deliberately relevant, not accidental.** The agent connected donations, raw `balanceOf` pricing and depositor loss. Internal accounting addresses the same pricing mechanism used by the profitable multi-depositor exploit, even when the agent misunderstood the eventual beneficiary and severity.
- **A recommendation is not a validated patch.** No implemented repair was required for the agent to establish an audit finding. Conversely, suggesting a repair does not establish that it was implemented, that every entry point was covered, or that the exploit is impossible afterward.
- **The original runs did not explain the full profitable multi-depositor exploit.** F0's later human-prompted follow-ups did. Recognizing victim loss and recommending a relevant repair deserve explicit credit, but the unsupported griefing-only conclusion remains a material audit-quality limitation.
- **Formal verification supplied useful additional coverage; it did not automatically supply the missing economic interpretation.** Relevant depositor properties, scope, models and assumptions determined what a failure or pass meant.

## Study and evidence boundaries

- The study compared Foundry tests/fuzzing (T) and Certora/CVL (F), with code-only, moderate README and explicit economic requirements as input levels. The initial Foundry pilot's exact T0 input identity remains unconfirmed.
- Each run produced informal properties, executable checks or CVL/model files, and a report. Compilation, prover execution and subsequent interpretation occurred in distinct phases; relevant proofs were run and feedback returned, rather than every possible rule being completed.
- This is a small qualitative pilot series, not a measured success-rate benchmark. Different outputs are evidence of specific capabilities and failure modes; they do not establish a general ranking between tools or models.
- Original reports, ordinary execution feedback, additional challenges and evaluator calibration remain separate evidence. The control V1 repair and its proof jobs are evaluator work, not an unprompted agent patch.
- This synthesis draws on retained sources and prior recorded reviews. The original F01 report/CVL are unavailable for fresh inspection; the subsequently supplied F0/F02 bundle was only partly reviewed. No new test or prover execution was performed for this document.

## Which fixes address which promises?

- **Internal accounting:** price wrapper shares from tTokens accepted through accounted entry points, rather than every tToken appearing in the wrapper's balance. A direct donation then does not automatically raise the accounted share price. The physical token balance still exists and must reconcile with accounting.
- **Account for actual receipts:** `_deposit` must use the tTokens actually obtained from Takara; `wrap` must account for actual incoming tTokens; exits must decrement consistently. Merely incrementing a counter from requested USDC or an unchecked nominal amount is not the same fix. Zero-share/zero-receipt and rounding policy still need their own requirements.
- **Larger decimals offset:** adds virtual share units and improves rounding precision. Offset 6 means `10^6` virtual share units, not six virtual shares. It does not make a price based on live `balanceOf` invariant to donations.
- **The original “and/or” wording needs review:** T02 lists internal accounting and/or an offset as a fix direction. These are not interchangeable for donation-neutral pricing; the offset does not remove the raw-balance dependency. Credit the relevant accounting insight without treating every alternative in the recommendation as sufficient.
- **Reject zero shares:** prevents that particular total-loss acceptance path. It does not establish fair value for positive-share deposits, nor prevent every profitable rounding sequence.
- **Minimum shares/slippage:** can protect a depositor who supplies an independently meaningful floor. A floor derived from an already manipulated preview does not independently establish fair value.
- These are different guarantees: donation-neutral pricing, bounded victim loss and absence of attacker profit. Failure of one is not automatically proof of all the others. Even the combination needs implementation review and checks against the intended scope.

## What the actual repair evidence establishes

- **Zero-share rejection is demonstrably insufficient in the reported F0 follow-up.** Four victims each receiving one share still give the attacker **+122.98 USDC** under the rejection-only variant. The same report gives **+110.049998 USDC** in a two-victim execution against the original wrapper. These are reported py-evm executions, not independent replays by this research review; the underlying scripts/raw execution logs were not supplied. [Execution report and table](../runs/F0/P8_EXPLOIT_REPORT.md).
- **Offset 6 had encouraging bounded evidence, not a universal safety proof.** The F0 follow-up reports **0 profitable cases in 20,000 sampled sequences** for that configuration, and no profit in the later listed bytecode cases. The sampled search bounds were donations up to 100,000 USDC and up to 12 victims. These results neither prove the general repair sufficient nor prove that it remains exploitable. [Search scope/results](../runs/F0/P8_FOLLOWUP.md), [bytecode cases and caveat](../runs/F0/P8_EXPLOIT_REPORT.md).
- **The calibration V1 used internal accounting, not an offset-only repair.** Its donation-neutrality rule D1 passed non-vacuously. Its fair-deposit P1 initially failed from an unreachable state with zero shares and an enormous tracked balance. That result did not demonstrate an executable attack against the repaired implementation. Pinned V1 description, jobs 4–5.
- **The supporting invariant matters as much as the green result.** P1 passed on V1 under `tracked tTokens == wrapper share supply`; Foundry exercised the invariant over 128,000 calls, but CVL assumed it rather than proving it inductively. Applying the analogous backing/supply equality to vulnerable V0 also produced a non-vacuous pass, because it excluded donation states. Jobs 8–9 and limitations.
- **Therefore do not present “FV shows the vulnerability always remains, even with offsets” as a result of this experiment.** The recorded offset evidence is bounded; the repaired-V1 failure concerned reachability; and a donation-neutrality violation alone would not establish profitable exploitation.
- **The underlying market is a separate boundary.** The calibration also found an empty-Takara-market donation/zero-tToken path that a wrapper-only accounting repair does not remove. The experiment seeded that market to its intended live context; broader deployment guarantees must state that assumption. [Recorded calibration finding](findings-log.md#calibration-reference-tests-before-any-agent-run).

## Overall experiment results and interpretation

- **Stronger economic requirements improved protective coverage.** T01's nonzero-share requirement can reject zero-share loss but says nothing about fair value for positive shares. T02's supplied R5 and F02's S5 check immediate redeemable value against the asset amount minus ε = 10 USDC base units. This is a materially stronger depositor promise. [T01 property](../runs/T1/PROPERTIES.md), [T02 tests](../runs/T2/WrapperProperties.t.sol#L151), [F02 `fairDeposit`](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L210).
- **Execution exposed loss without guaranteeing correct interpretation.** T01 reports 25 passing/9 failing tests; T02 reports 34 passing/10 failing tests out of 44. Failing counts are not counts of distinct vulnerabilities. T02's final summary reports a positive-share fair-deposit loss of 349 base units against ε = 10, and a 1,000-USDC zero-share loss. The initial report's corresponding small-loss figure was 358; that discrepancy remains unresolved. [T01 report](../runs/T1/REPORT.md), [T02 final results](../runs/T2/SUMMARY.md).
- **Useful repair advice appeared before execution.** T02's initial report and F02's compile-only report already contain internal-accounting advice. We therefore cannot attribute the discovery of that repair to proof execution or treat it as a benefit unique to formal verification.
- **Translation quality improved in some places and regressed in others.** F02's S5 becomes a useful asset-denominated lower bound. Its S19 selector implications omit caller permission, and I1 restricts an informal all-positive-input claim to inputs above ε. Better supplied intent did not uniformly produce faithful CVL. [S19 informal requirement](../runs/F2/PROPERTIES.md), [selector-only rule](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L424), [domain restriction](../runs/F2/certora/specs/SpectraWrappedTakara.spec#L222).
- **Formal verification changed coverage and the supporting evidence.** Calibration P1 examined a symbolic post-donation state without generating a donation sequence; F0's follow-ups reported conditions for non-dust zero-share loss and code-to-arithmetic checks. Stateful fuzzing needs donation actions in its handler to explore that transition. Symbolic coverage creates a corresponding obligation to justify starting-state reachability. Calibration P1, [F0 proof report](../runs/F0/P8_EXPLOIT_REPORT.md), [T02 handler comparison](../runs/T2/WrapperInvariants.t.sol#L202).
- **Recognition did not guarantee a protective rule.** F01's recorded report recognized donations, but its property/rule set omitted fair-value and donation-neutrality obligations. This is a property-selection gap, separate from whether a selected rule was translated correctly. [Recorded F01 review](F1.md#discovery-and-interpretation).
- **The griefing-only generalization survived stronger specifications and solver feedback.** A one-victim attacker loss establishes one unprofitable sequence. Multiple victims can add value to the attacker's existing claim until the attack becomes profitable. Formal victim-loss counterexamples do not automatically establish that net-profit sequence or an audit severity. [F02 original interpretation](../runs/F2/REPORT.md), [F02 post-proof interpretation](../runs/F2/FOLLOWUP_REPORT_F02.md), [F0 later correction](../runs/F0/P8_FOLLOWUP.md).

## Hypotheses: what is supported and what remains limited

- **H1 — AI property discovery, translation, investigation and interpretation:** supplied economic intent coincided with better victim-protection properties, and agents translated some requirements usefully. Findings challenge a blanket expectation of good result interpretation: repeated griefing-only reasoning, authorization omissions and reachability issues persisted. Early internal-accounting advice adds a positive capability that vulnerability-verdict-only scoring would miss. One run per condition does not establish that AI is generally bad at informal specifications or reliably good at formal translation.
- **H2 — FV and testing/fuzzing provide complementary coverage:** supported by arbitrary-state checks, donation-handler dependence, arithmetic proof obligations, and the need for executable traces to validate economic impact. The evidence does not establish that FV generally finds more bugs, that every failed proof is exploitable, or that tests can replace all useful proof obligations.
- **Remediation recommendation, exploit comprehension and repair validation must be recorded separately.** Their separation explains the observed results without changing the primary audit criterion into a checklist of unrelated partial successes.

## Final audit assessment and practical workflow

- **Primary finding quality:** credit demonstrated knowledge of the mechanism and exploitation conditions, material damage and justified severity. A precise profitable-exploit explanation is stronger than a griefing-only account of the same root cause. No implemented fix is required to make the finding valid.
- **Partial finding with relevant remediation:** the appropriate description for T02/F02's donation findings is mechanism/victim-loss recognition with incomplete economic interpretation, alongside a deliberately relevant internal-accounting recommendation. It is inaccurate to say either that the agent understood the full attack or that it merely stumbled upon an unrelated repair.
- **Broader finding deliberately covering the target:** fixing donation-sensitive accounting can address both griefing and the profitable variant. That shared root cause merits credit even when the severity analysis is incomplete. An unrelated change that accidentally blocks the target would be a different category; an unimplemented proposal should never be labeled “patched.”
- **Severity:** distinguish disagreement over a label from missing a material damage scenario. T01's Low griefing assessment and T02's High assessment show that labels can differ even while both miss multi-victim profit. Actual integration/exposure and prerequisites determine severity; attacker profit does not automatically imply Critical.
- **Review order:** establish the informal protection and economic scope; inspect the exact test/CVL assertion and input domain; review external models/assumptions; classify execution status; validate counterexample reachability; calculate victim damage and attacker net across independent deposits; then evaluate and verify the proposed repair.
- **For patch verification:** retain original requirements, test zero-share and positive-share loss, cover all accounting entry/exit points and direct donations, and justify supporting invariants. Keep donation neutrality, asset-loss bounds and attacker-profit claims distinct.
- **Presentation takeaway:** AI can identify a useful repair direction while misunderstanding why the issue is economically dangerous. FV can strengthen selected guarantees, but reviewing specifications, models, economic interpretation and repair evidence remains essential.
