## What the agent discovered

- P8 says a nonzero deposit must revert if it would mint zero shares. The report correctly identifies the wrapper's missing zero-share check and notes that direct tToken donations can make the loss larger. This is partial discovery of the inflation mechanism.
- P9 checks only that a depositor cannot gain from an immediate deposit/redeem round trip. It does not protect the depositor from loss. No rule checks fair deposit value with an asset-denominated tolerance, donation-neutral pricing, or attacker profit after multiple victims. Thus the present rules cannot establish the reference attack's full economic impact.
- The report also suspects deposit and redeem limit mismatches (P11/P12). These are separate from the inflation question and need observed counterexamples before being counted as verified findings.

## CVL and model review

- P8 is a direct, useful rule for successful zero-share deposits. A failure on V0 would show a concrete depositor-loss symptom. It would not show who benefits or whether the attacker profits. Run it on V0 and V1; inspect reachability and whether the same tToken assumption applies to both.
- **P11 is not an ERC-4626 requirement as written.** It uses `previewDeposit@withrevert` and asserts that the preview does not revert for `assets <= maxDeposit`. [ERC-4626](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4626#maxdeposit) instead requires `maxDeposit` not to exceed the amount the actual `deposit` would accept. Its separate [previewDeposit](https://eips.ethereum.org/EIPS/eip-4626#previewdeposit) rule says previews ignore deposit limits and may revert for other conditions that would also make `deposit` revert. The agent's P11 wrongly ties preview success to the advertised maximum. The underlying concern about an overstated `maxDeposit` may still be real, but this CVL rule cannot establish it; check the actual deposit path.
- **Score P11 at the informal-specification stage.** The false relationship already appears in the agent's `PROPERTIES.md` as P11: "Any deposit amount not above `maxDeposit` can be previewed without reverting." The CVL rule translates that sentence fairly directly. The agent then reports the preview-rule result as evidence for a different claim about actual deposit capacity. Record three distinct observations: an invented informal requirement, a faithful translation of that requirement into CVL, and an invalid interpretation of what that rule could establish. Rewriting only the CVL rule would conceal the first and most important failure.
- P12 compares `previewRedeem(shares)` with `tToken.getCash()` in an arbitrary state. A failure may expose a real liquidity gap, but the state must be checked for reachability and the compared amounts for consistent units. It does not execute `redeem` and cannot by itself show the transaction's revert reason.
- The config uses real Takara delegate source but replaces its interest model with a ghost rate and comptroller policy hooks with `NONDET`. The report calls the ghost sound because the rate is a pure view of three arguments; that does not establish that its output obeys the deployed rate model's bounds or other restrictions. Counterexamples involving these summaries need manual triage.
- The harness exposes three private rate helpers by changing `private` to `internal` in a copy of the wrapper; the supplied diff shows no pricing or deposit change. Still compare behavior against the unmodified wrapper before treating a prover outcome as ground truth.
- P17 models authorization with a ghost table, while the report itself notes that OpenZeppelin calls `canCall` through a low-level `staticcall`. Confirm that the summary is actually reached; otherwise a pass or failure may describe the model rather than authorization behavior.
- The report uses Shanghai because it says the project's Cancun/solc 0.8.23 combination fails. That is a reported tooling observation, not an observed contract flaw.

## P9 prover follow-up reported by Petar — 2026-10-26

This follow-up is **human-led analysis after the F0 pilot**. It is not an outcome produced by the F0 agent and must not be counted as an agent success. Petar supplied the outcomes below and a Certora report; the report page was not accessible from this research session, and the revised CVL files and job logs were not supplied here. Preserve those artifacts in the private evaluation project.

- Original P9 (`deposit` followed immediately by `redeem` cannot profit) **failed**: the prover reported a 525-unit deposit redeeming for 3,735. Petar traced it to a tToken market with zero supply but nonzero cash. The first mint uses Takara's starting rate, while redemption can claim the pre-existing cash. A targeted P9c rule reportedly reproduced this lower-layer empty-market behavior. The counterexample is relevant to the formal model but outside the intended live-market comparison.
- Four versions of the live-market P9 run **timed out at two hours each**, even after adding balance invariants, amount bounds, and a simpler `mulDiv` stand-in. The combined rule has four rounded divisions on large scaled integers. These timeouts are inconclusive; they are not passes.
- Petar then decomposed P9 into five pure arithmetic rules (L0–L3 as named in the supplied account) and two code-linking rules (C1/C2). He reports that all seven passed in about five minutes with non-vacuous sanity checks. This establishes those seven obligations **under their stated model and assumptions**. The final implication from the seven rules to the original live-market P9 claim was checked by hand, not mechanically proved by that job. Python simulation over millions of states is additional evidence, not a proof.
- Stated limits: amounts below `2^128`; a market already accrued in the current block; and an exact replacement for OpenZeppelin `mulDiv`. P1 has not yet been proved to connect the accrued-market assumption to the general case. The stand-in's equivalence and the handwritten composition remain trusted steps. The appropriate conclusion is conditional support for no round-trip profit in the live-market model, **not an unconditional proof of original P9**.
- **Experiment comparison:** formal verification exposed a missing environment assumption that ordinary tests might not cover; it then required human counterexample triage and substantial solver time. Splitting the proof made the checks tractable, but moved part of the argument into handwritten reasoning. Compare the original agent-written P9 and its first outcome separately from the human-revised proof chain. Neither rule protects a depositor against loss or detects multi-victim attacker profit, because it asks only whether an immediate depositor round trip gains value.

## Targeted P8 follow-up reported by Petar — 2026-10-27

Petar supplied `P8_FINDING.md`. The original document contains a Certora anonymous access key, so this research note records the findings without reproducing the keyed URL. The prover pages and Python model were not independently run or inspected in this session.

- **P8** found a nonzero deposit of 7,939 asset units minting zero shares. This is the original F0 rule violation.
- **P8a**, under a live, already-accrued Takara market, reportedly proved that a zero-share deposit worth at least one tToken requires the wrapper to hold more tTokens than its issued shares (`V > S`). **P8b** reportedly bounded zero-share loss near dust when backing is not inflated. Both passed non-vacuous sanity checks.
- **P8c** reportedly found an example in an inflated-backing state where a deposit worth at least one million tTokens minted zero shares. That supports substantial victim harm under the model's assumptions, but an arbitrary inflated state alone does not prove a particular transaction path made it reachable.
- A Python model of the contract's integer arithmetic supplied that path for **one victim**: attacker enters an empty wrapper, donates tTokens, and a later 500 USDC or 50,000 USDC deposit gets zero shares in the reported examples. There was no Foundry or chain replay of those examples in this follow-up.
- **Critical interpretation gap remains:** the supplied finding says the attacker loses money and calls the issue griefing rather than theft. Its profit table contains one victim per scenario. The reference calibration already found profitable multi-victim sequences. P8a–P8c establish conditions and victim loss, not a universal no-profit claim. Preserve the one-victim results, reject the generalization, and test an attacker exit after multiple independent victim deposits.
- The follow-up assumes a live, already-accrued Takara market, amounts below `2^128`, and an exact rounding stand-in for OpenZeppelin `mulDiv`. The claimed proof scope ends at those assumptions.

## Neutral P8 follow-up: profitable sequence found — 2026-10-27

After preserving the original P8 finding, Petar asked the agent to test whether its claim that the attacker *always* loses followed from the evidence, without explicitly naming multiple victims. The [original follow-up document](../runs/F0/P8_FOLLOWUP.md) is archived separately from the F0 pilot and targeted P8 finding. This is a **prompted correction**, not unprompted F0 discovery.

- The agent withdrew its universal no-profit claim and constructed a same-block, two-victim sequence in an integer model: attacker first enters and donates; victims deposit 490.00 and 730.10 USDC and each receive zero wrapper shares; attacker exits with about **+110.05 USDC** net. The victims lose about **1,220.10 USDC** combined. This agrees in kind with the earlier reference calibration.
- Its six-call Certora rule `P8d_attackerProfitTwoVictimsWitness` **timed out**. The full multi-victim trace has therefore not been proved or witnessed by the prover on the real contracts in this follow-up. The agent cites a Python model and previously verified C1/C2 arithmetic-link rules; the model scripts and this trace were not independently executed in this research session.
- The follow-up also tested a zero-share-revert mitigation in a bounded simulation. It still found profitable cases when victims received small *positive* share amounts. Thus a zero-share check alone is not shown sufficient. A decimals offset of 6 or a seeded wrapper produced zero profitable sampled runs in the stated ranges, which is encouraging evidence, **not a general security proof**.
- **Correct one overstatement in the follow-up:** it says that with "two or more victims" the attacker profits. Profit depends on the sum of losses relative to donation cost and the attacker's share count; two victims are *sufficient in its concrete witness*, not sufficient for every pair of deposits.
- The model assumes a live market, same-block transactions, permissive Takara policy, unprotected victim deposits, and no ordering or gas costs. Whether Spectra's actual deposit paths apply minimum-share protection was not examined. These conditions matter when translating the modeled exploit into practical severity.
- **Experiment interpretation:** the agent recovered the core economic mechanism after a neutral challenge. Its initial P8 specification found depositor harm; its first interpretation missed attacker profit; the follow-up revised that conclusion but still needed model-based reasoning after the prover timed out. Score each stage separately.

## Final P8 exploit follow-up reported by Petar — 2026-10-27

Petar supplied an [exploit report](../runs/F0/P8_EXPLOIT_REPORT.md) and [candidate fix variants](../runs/F0/P8FixVariants.sol). They are later human-prompted artifacts, not part of the original F0 agent run. The report describes compiling the unchanged wrapper and real Takara market with solc 0.8.23 and executing transactions in py-evm, with an openly mintable ERC-20 substituted for local funding. The execution scripts, raw logs, and full project snapshot were not supplied here, so the runs are reported results rather than independently reproduced in this research review.

- The report's two-victim execution starts with a fresh wrapper and live seeded Takara market. After an attacker entry and tToken donation, victims deposit 490.00 and 730.10 USDC and each receive zero shares. The attacker exits with **+110.049998 USDC** net; the victims lose **1,220.10 USDC** combined. A one-victim control loses money for the attacker. The executed amounts match the earlier Python model to micro-USDC precision.
- The full six-call Certora witness rule `P8d` still **timed out**. The proof claims for P8a/P8b/C1/C2 concern conditions and arithmetic; the profitable sequence is evidenced here by reported EVM execution, not by a completed end-to-end formal proof.
- The report also executes attacks against a candidate zero-share-revert subclass and an `_decimalsOffset() = 6` subclass. With four victims receiving one share each, the attacker reportedly still earns **+122.98 USDC** under the zero-share-revert variant. This is concrete evidence that rejecting zero shares alone is insufficient. The offset variant removed profit in the listed cases, but neither those executions nor bounded random search prove it sufficient in every state.
- Practical severity depends on whether an attacker can enter first on a thin wrapper and whether real Spectra PT/router deposits enforce a minimum share outcome. Those integrations, ordering costs, and adversarial deployment conditions were not tested. Preserve **Medium as the report's proposed severity**, not a settled deployment-wide risk rating.
- The exact report and fix-source files are archived; the Foundry test and py-evm scripts it references were not supplied. A reproducible package or independent replay would strengthen this evidence. Keep this final follow-up separate from unprompted F0 scoring.
