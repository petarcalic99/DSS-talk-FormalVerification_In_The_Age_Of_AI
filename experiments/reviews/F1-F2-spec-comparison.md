## Correction: F01 did consider donation attacks

- **Do not read a missing dedicated property as absent attack analysis.** The [F01 review](F1.md#discovery-and-interpretation) explicitly records report **B-5** discussing donation-driven zero-share loss and classifying it as griefing. F01 therefore recognized the mechanism before receiving F02's explicit L2 economic requirements. The omission is in protective formal obligations, not all consideration of the attack.
- **Donation-inflated states were not shown to be excluded.** The unchanged F01 mock inherits ERC-20 transfers, and the earlier source review found no setup equality forcing wrapper backing to equal issued shares. Its ordinary preview and conversion checks can remain satisfied when backing is inflated. That is different from a rule asserting that donations cannot harm a depositor, or an executed donation-attack test.
- **Evidence limit of this recheck.** The original F01 report/CVL upload paths are no longer available, and no F01 result package was found in the control repository's result paths. This correction relies on the earlier source review and the available unchanged mock; it does not claim a fresh independent reread of the missing originals or evidence of a donation-specific prover run.

## Good, bad and missing aspects

- **Good: central economic coverage improves.** F02 preserves supplied donation neutrality and fair deposit, writes appropriately shaped rules, and identifies a large victim loss. F01 had already recognized the donation mechanism; the earlier review records no dedicated protective requirement. F02 should receive credit for explicit economic coverage, not first recognition of donations. The reported pass/fail contrast between preview consistency and fair value is especially useful for the research.
- **Good: several existing translations remain competent.** Receiver balance deltas, pause rejection, disabled operations and caller comparisons use appropriate checks. Their common obligations are mostly equivalent across stages; rewriting them should not be scored as a new translation improvement.
- **Bad: authorization becomes a different, weaker proposition.** F01 asks whether the successful caller is permitted. F02 asks whether the selected function is the one that changes state, yet the agent labels access control verified. This is the clearest same-intent translation regression.
- **Bad: successful-path accounting is sometimes overread as security.** Preview equality, no-gain round trips and balance conservation can hold while a user loses money. Their passes are useful only with the actual guarantee stated. Neither the old nor new version establishes profitable-attack absence.
- **Missing: coverage is lost as well as added.** F02 has no actual within-limit redemption-success rule, no dedicated exact wrap/unwrap preview pair and no dedicated owner/other-holder burn-isolation rule. F01's redemption rule already omitted approved callers; F02 does not close that gap.
- **Missing: full economic interpretation and final reproducibility.** Both stages miss profitable multi-victim impact. F02 additionally needs the exact revised spec and exported traces to audit reported results, including its suspicious zero-price witness and broad “holds” labels. Proof execution is not itself a correction of the informal claim or its interpretation.
- **Research conclusion.** The improvement is strongest in explicit economic intent becoming an executable obligation. General CVL quality and model fidelity do not improve uniformly. Compare property selection, matched translation, modeled scope, observed/reported result and agent interpretation separately; F01 compile-only versus F02's repaired follow-up is not a controlled solver-performance comparison.
